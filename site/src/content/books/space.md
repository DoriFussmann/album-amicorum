## An endless sky to dream under

The Space edition wraps the book in soft, celestial watercolor — muted teal and cream, stars and
planets painted by hand rather than printed bright. It's the quiet end of space: not rockets and
noise, but the feeling of lying back and looking up. For the child who asks what's out there, this is
the world they'll want to hand to their friends.

## Made to be written in

Inside, the same gentle illustrations frame open, cream-paper pages with room for friends to leave
what matters: a handwritten memory, a drawing, a favorite thing, a message meant just for them.
Nothing is filled in ahead of time. Every page waits for a real person to complete it, which is why
no two finished books are ever alike.

## A keepsake, not a notebook

A notebook gets used up and thrown away. This is meant to be kept. As friends add to it across a
school year — or a whole childhood — it becomes a record of who was there and what they meant, in
their own handwriting. Years later, the handwriting is the point.

## A gift that isn't a screen

The Space edition suits the child with a big imagination and the moments worth marking — a birthday,
the last week of school, a move, a friendship finding its feet. It asks for nothing to charge and
nothing to log into. Just a pen, a few friends, and some time.
